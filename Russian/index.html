Pronunciation

Rules

  • In Russian, you use the front part of your mouth, with your teeth closer together than in English.
  • There are 10 vowel letters, and they affect how the consonant before them is pronounced. There are hard and soft vowels.
Hard vowels
  • А
  • Э
  • Ы
  • О
  • У
Soft vowels
  • Я
  • Е
  • И
  • Ё
  • Ю

There are 6 consonant exceptions that are always hard or soft (ignoring the vowel rule).

Always hard consonants
  • Ж
  • Ш
  • Ц
Always soft consonants
  • Ч
  • Щ
  • Й

2. Greetings

In the present tense there is no verb to be. For example if someone asks your name, in Russia you would say the equivalent to "I Alex". In Russian is "Я Алекс".

  • Привет (Hello, only for friends and known people) (Its pronounced as in Preview, vet, Yet) (prevyet).
  • Здравствуйте (Hello, for strangers, older people, or formal situations) (Its pronounced as in Zoo, drive, father, stop, vet, we, see, tea, yet) (zdrahst-vwee-tye).
  • Очень приятно (Nice to meet you) (Its pronounced as in go, cheese, yet, net, preview, yard, stop, net, about) (oh-chen pree-yat-na).
  • Как Ты (How are you?) Remember no "To be", so, (How you?) (Its pronounced as in car, father, key, tea, bit) (kahk tih).
Note about vowel reduction: In Russian, vowels that are not emphasized change sound.
  • O (not emphasized) sounds like "a" (as in "about")
  • E (not emphasized) sounds like "i" (as in "it")
  • Я (not emphasized) sounds like "i" (as in "it")
That's why the second part of "Очень" is pronounced "chin" instead of "chen". And that's why the final O in "приятно" is pronounced "a": (not "pree-yat-no").
Note about "Как ты": In Russian, "Как ты?" is informal. Only use it with friends, family, or people you know well. It is a genuine question, not a casual greeting like in English. With strangers, it sounds too familiar and personal — it's better to simply not ask.

Gender of Nouns
There are more rules, but the next ones will simplify them for you.
  • Words that in singular end with a consonant are masculine
  • Words that end on a or я are feminine
  • Words that end on о or е are neutral
Exceptions:
  • Feminine words that refer to someone of the masculine gender, are masculine. Example: Папа (It means dad)
  • Nouns ending in ь can be either masculine or feminine.
